# AER network tariff files vs distributor-published price lists, FY2023-24 to FY2026-27

Repository: `yehezkieled/aer-tariff-recon`. Everything is reproducible with one command from the repository root: `./run.sh` (creates `.venv`, fetches any source document not in the checkout from the URL recorded in `sources/inventory.csv`, parses the AER files and every distributor file, reconciles, and assembles this report).

Deliverables:

- `REPORT.md` - this report.
- `discrepancies.csv` - every discrepancy row (value differs, AER-only / distributor-only tariff codes and components) with its explanation class.
- Regenerated by `./run.sh` into `out/` (not committed): `discrepancies.xlsx` (adds the full comparison, the summary grid, the format timeline and the source inventory as sheets), `recon_detail.csv` (every compared pair), `match_grid.csv`, `format_timeline.csv`, `recon_summary.json`, `aer_long.csv` (AER side, normalised), `dnsp/*.csv` (distributor side, normalised), and `report.html` (portable report page).

Conventions: all comparisons are of the **total network price (NUoS)**, GST exclusive, in standard units (cents; fixed charges per day; demand charges per published billing period). Claims not backed by a document or a command output are marked `[UNSURE]`.

## 1. Summary

- **The "AER report" is not one thing.** There is no AER-authored price file for 2023-24; 2024-25 has 14 per-distributor stakeholder workbooks with a distribution / transmission / jurisdictional split; 2025-26 and 2026-27 have one consolidated workbook with total prices only. Section 2 gives exact URLs, versions and dates.
- **Rate comparison.** Of 6600 price components compared across 50 distributor-year cells, 5369 are identical and 852 agree within published rounding precision; 379 differ, including 379 unexplained differences. [UNSURE] Metering and ACT LFiT are possible causes where the documents mention them; their component adjustments have not been substantiated and are excluded from documented-explanation totals.
- **Content differs.** The AER files list 106 tariff codes the distributors do not publish (mostly zero-priced placeholders for new trial tariffs and withdrawn tariffs still carried by the AER) and omit 335 codes the distributors price (site-specific SA Power Networks variants, Jemena and AusNet large-business tariffs, trial and obsolete codes). The 2024-25 AER file for SA Power Networks has code typos (ZSN/ZSS and STN/STR swapped on five site-specific codes, a blank code "-") and all-zero rows for SAPN's new Electrify/Flexible tariffs. The AER files omit SA Power Networks' Diversify rebate, AusNet's premium feed-in credit, Energex Band 5 and Ergon Excess Demand components.
- **Format changed on both sides every year** (section 5.3): AER layout, labels and units changed in 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27; nearly every distributor changed document type or table structure at least once in the period.
- **Coverage gaps**: 6 cells could not be compared because the distributor's own document was not retrievable or has no price table (section 3); 3 further cells use the AER-hosted copy of the distributor's document as the distributor side and are flagged.

## 3. Distributor sources and gaps

Every distributor file, its exact URL and how it was obtained is in the inventory table at the end (also `sources/inventory.csv` and the "Sources" sheet of the xlsx). Cloudflare blocks automated access to energex.com.au, ergon.com.au, powerwater.com.au and parts of essentialenergy.com.au; those files were taken from the Wayback Machine with the archive URL recorded. Nothing was behind a login. Gaps that could not be closed:

- **No distributor-side copy for 2023-24** (so nothing to compare; the AER-hosted copy is the only source): Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, Essential Energy, Powercor.
- **CitiPower 2025-26 and United Energy 2025-26**: the only retrievable documents (Final Pricing PDFs) contain no price table; they refer to a "Tariff Summary" workbook that was neither archived nor hosted by the AER.
- **United Energy 2024-25**: own-site copy returns 403 and is not in Wayback; the AER-hosted copy of UE's Tariff Summary workbook stands in (flagged on the grid).
- **AusNet Services 2024-25**: AusNet's own Annual Pricing Proposal PDF has no tariff schedule ("Schedule of tariffs are shown in Attachment 5", not retrievable) - only tariff-trial prices; the AER-hosted Schedule of Tariffs xlsx stands in (flagged).
- **Power and Water 2026-27**: own-site SCS tariff PDF blocked and not archived; the AER-hosted 2026-27 pricing proposal stands in (flagged).
- **SA Power Networks 2025-26**: the standalone Tariff Price List was superseded in place and is not archived; the price table in SAPN's pricing proposal overview (Table 9) is used.

## 4. Method

1. `scripts/parse_aer.py` reads the AER files into one long table (`out/aer_long.csv`, one row per tariff code x component x basis). Zero-priced AER rows (TBA / placeholder codes) are kept as "(no non-zero components)".
2. One parser per distributor group (`scripts/dnsp/*.py`, contract in `scripts/dnsp/CONTRACT.md`) reads every distributor document into the same schema (`scripts/schema.py`): distributor, FY, code, name, class, component, charge type, time band, season, unit, value, standard value/unit, GST basis, component basis (NUoS/DUoS/TUoS/JSA/DPPC), source file, source URL, note. Parsers emit every basis the document publishes and GST-exclusive prices (GST-inclusive repeats are cross-checked, not compared).
3. `scripts/reconcile.py` compares, per distributor and year, the AER-authored file (2024-25 onward) or the AER-hosted document (2023-24) against the distributor's own publication, NUoS basis, in standard units (`scripts/units.py`: money to cents, fixed charges per day, demand per published period, with AER seasonal suffixes such as "highsn"/"lowsn"/"Summer" read as the season of a per-day price; a billing period inferred only from a label is treated as soft because e.g. SA Power Networks bills "annual demand" per day).
   - Tariff codes are matched exactly, then by alias (AER joint codes "A100/F100" and "010, 011*", trailing "*", "-SA" suffix, Ergon's T1-T4 region suffix), then by tariff name (similarity >= 0.9 with identical digits). A distributor code that is one half of a joint AER code is compared against the joint row.
   - Components must agree on charge type, export charge/reward direction, time band, season and physical quantity before either value or label matching; a demand price whose document does not establish kW or kVA is compared against either and flagged "[UNSURE] unit unverified". Known conflicting billing periods cannot match. Established label identities are normalized by the parsers; a unique fully compatible counterpart is matched regardless of label similarity.
   - Compared pairs are `equal`, `equal_after_rounding`, or `value_differs`. Rounding uses decimal precision preserved from PDF tokens and spreadsheet display formats, with numeric conversion performed separately. Unverified causes remain `unexplained`; mentions of metering or LFiT are hypotheses, not documented component adjustments. Unmatched codes and components remain separate discrepancy rows.
   - Match rate = exact-or-rounding share of compared components; reconciled rate = share whose difference is zero, rounding, or explained.
4. `scripts/write_report.py` renders `out/report.md` and `out/report.html`; `scripts/report_tables.py` renders the per-cell tables embedded below.

Reproduction command: `./run.sh`. Generated comparison tables follow in section 8.

## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Verified comparisons and unverified causes

Only identical prices and differences within published rounding precision count as reconciled. Component matching requires compatible charge type, export charge/reward direction, time band, season and physical quantity; kWh and kVAh, and kW and kVA, remain distinct except where the distributor document does not establish the demand quantity (flagged unit unverified).

[UNSURE] Embedded metering and ACT LFiT may contribute to some differences. A document mentioning either does not establish the adjustment for a particular component. These hypotheses remain `unexplained` in the CSV and are excluded from documented-explanation totals. Uniform offsets are not promoted to explanations.

Demand periods inferred from labels remain marked with `?`; this inference does not establish a billing period. Unmatched components and coverage limitations are listed in the generated detail below.

### 5.2 Genuine content differences between the AER file and the distributor publication

The generated detail lists unmatched codes and components and published placeholders. An unmatched row does not by itself prove that a rate is missing: uncertain labels, units or component identities can prevent comparison. The examples below describe publication content; full per-cell lists appear in section 8 and in `discrepancies.csv`.

1. **Unpriced placeholder rows in the AER files.** The AER lists tariffs with all-zero prices that the distributor prices in full: Energex trial tariffs (AER "TBA8/TBA10/TBA11/TBA13", "1000", "HSAC", "CSAC" vs Energex 92100/92200/94100/94200 and the CAC 3000/4000/4500 in 2024-25), Ergon trial and transitional codes (ETBA*/WTBA*/MTBA*), SA Power Networks 2024-25 new "Electrify"/"Flexible" tariffs (RELE, RELE2W, SBTOUE, HVADF, LBADF, LBGF, HVBGF, STNF, STNGF, ZSNF, ZSNGF all zero in the AER file, priced in SAPN's list), AusNet 2025-26 (10 placeholder codes), CitiPower/Powercor/United Energy 2026-27 (one or two each), Essential 2025-26/2026-27 (BLNP1AO).
2. **Tariffs missing from the AER file but published and priced by the distributor** (`dnsp_only_code` without a placeholder): Jemena 2024-25 and 2025-26 large-business codes A34E/A34M/A34T/A37C/A40E/A40R/A40T/A480/A48C/A500/A50A/A50C/A50E/A50M/A50T/A50X (the AER stakeholder report lists 24 joint codes against Jemena's 50/40); AusNet 2024-25 NSP82/NSP91/NSP94/NSP95 sub-transmission critical-peak tariffs; SA Power Networks 2025-26 and 2026-27 site-specific locational variants (HVADnnn, LBADnnn, STNnnn/STRnnn, ZSNnnn/ZSSnnn: 44 and 29 codes) that the consolidated report does not carry at all although the 2024-25 per-distributor file did; Essential 2025-26/2026-27 BLNBEX1/BLNREX2/BLNE24AU/BLNE25AU; the Victorian distributors' trial and closed codes; Ausgrid's trial and local-use-of-system tariffs (EA955/EA956/EA964/EA965 etc.); Endeavour 2024-25 obsolete combination codes (Table 3a, 23 codes priced identically to their parents).
3. **Tariffs in the AER file that the distributor no longer lists**: Ergon Energy 2025-26 - the AER consolidated report prices 22 withdrawn tariffs (names carry "withdrawn") absent from Ergon's price list; Energex 2025-26 three such codes; AusNet 2024-25 NASN2P/NASN2S (priced in the AER file, not in AusNet's schedule); SA Power Networks 2024-25 ZSS766; Essential BLNDTRS (2025-26/2026-27).
4. **Code and label mismatches for the same priced tariff**: SA Power Networks 2024-25 - the AER file prints ZSN035/ZSN272/ZSN273/STN483/ZSS951 where SAPN's price list prints ZSS035/ZSS272/ZSS273/STR483/ZSN951, uses "BCL" for SAPN's "M/QOPCL", suffixes every code "-SA", and has one priced row with code "-" (values equal SAPN's ZSN228); Ergon 2024-25 - the AER lists one code per tariff with the transmission region in "Other identifier" while Ergon publishes T1/T2/T3 codes; AER 2025-26 re-uses MDSTT4 for a current and a withdrawn Ergon tariff; Jemena/Evoenergy - AER joint codes "A100/F100", "010, 011*" versus separate distributor rows; Endeavour SL/TL/NW renamed ENSL/ENTL/ENNW in 2024-25; Victorian 2026-27 renames (CRTOU->CRSTOU, PRTOU->PRSTOU, URTOU->URSTOU, UnMet->UNMET, NDMO21->NDMO).
5. **Components present on one side only** (`*_only_component`): the AER 2024-25 template omits Energex "Band 5 Charge" and Ergon "Excess Demand Charge"; the AER files omit SA Power Networks' "Diversify" rebate (-0.33 $/day in 2024-25; Tier 1/Tier 2 -0.33/-0.55 $/day in 2025-26 and 2026-27) and AusNet's -60 c/kWh premium feed-in credit on the P codes (2024-25); the AER 2024-25 Essential file carries an "H Capacity" $/kVA component on the demand tariffs that Essential's price list does not show (9 rows); from 2025-26 the AER attaches Essential's export rebate and export charge (>7.5 kWh) to the import codes BLNRSS2/BLNBSS1 while Essential publishes them as separate export tariff codes BLNREX2/BLNBEX1; Evoenergy's critical-peak export charge/rebate is written in cents/kWh by the AER and c/kVAh by Evoenergy (same numbers); Evoenergy 2026-27 lists code 108/109 twice (second block 'LV Stand-Alone Small Battery Network', same prices).

## 6. Recommendations

1. Investigate unexplained differences component by component. [UNSURE] Metering and LFiT may contribute where the documents mention them; verify the published adjustment amount, charge type, units and applicability before applying it. A uniform offset alone does not establish the cause.
2. Treat the AER file's zero-priced rows as "not published" rather than "free": the distributor's own list is the only source for new trial/flexible tariffs in the year they start (Energex, Ergon, SA Power Networks 2024-25, AusNet 2025-26).
3. For large-business and site-specific tariffs (Jemena A34x-A50x, AusNet NSP82/91/94/95, SA Power Networks HVADnnn/ZSSnnn/STRnnn) the consolidated report is incomplete; use the distributor's list.
4. Use the code matching rules in `scripts/reconcile.py` (joint codes, "-SA", and Ergon-only T-region suffixes). Review unmatched codes and components individually; similarity and price agreement alone do not establish component identity.
5. The reconciliation is reproducible (`./run.sh`); when the AER publishes a new version (the 2025-26 and 2026-27 reports each went through five versions in the first four months of the year) re-run it against the versioned URL rather than the landing page. If this tooling is to be kept, commit `scripts/`, `sources/inventory.csv`, `notes/` and `run.sh`; the downloaded `sources/` files can be re-fetched from the recorded URLs except the Wayback copies, which should be committed.
